Explaining a move is not predicting it

Same-day correlation asks whether the stock and the factor move together today. Next-day correlation asks whether today's factor move tells you anything about tomorrow. Only the second helps a forecast.

4 · Feature evidence

Which data carries any signal?

Information coefficient (IC) is the rank correlation between a feature today and the stock's return over the next 5 days. Professionals treat an IC of 0.02–0.05 as useful if it holds up year after year. "Stable" is the share of years where the sign matched.

5 · Model report

Walk-forward results, year by year

AUC measures ranking skill: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. Accuracy must be compared with the "always guess up" baseline, because stocks rise more often than they fall.

6 · Volatility

How much it moves is predictable

Direction is close to a coin flip, but the size of the next move is not. Calm weeks tend to follow calm weeks and wild weeks follow wild weeks. The same features and the same walk-forward test predict next week's volatility well.

Annualized volatility, one point per week, out of sample. R² is measured on log volatility across all 8 stocks: the share of week-to-week variation the forecast explains. Useful for sizing positions and options pricing, even though it says nothing about direction.

7 · Large language model

Can Claude beat the model?

Claude gets the same numbers as the ML model, written as a short analyst briefing, and gives a probability that the stock rises over the next 5 days. To stop it from "remembering" the answers, only dates after its training-data cutoff are tested.
